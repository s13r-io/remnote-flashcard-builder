# World War II: Key Events and Facts

## What Started World War II?

World War II began on September 1, 1939, when Nazi Germany, led by Adolf Hitler, invaded Poland. Britain and France declared war on Germany two days later, on September 3, 1939. The war would last until 1945 and result in approximately 70-85 million deaths, making it the deadliest conflict in human history.

## Key Causes of World War II

The war had multiple causes. The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919 after World War I, imposed harsh penalties on Germany, creating resentment and economic hardship. Economic depression in the 1930s led to unemployment and social unrest across Europe. The rise of authoritarian leaders like Adolf Hitler in Germany, Benito Mussolini in Italy, and Hideki Tojo in Japan created militaristic regimes bent on expansion. Appeasement by Britain and France allowed aggressive nations to expand unchecked.

## The European Theater

The European Theater was the primary conflict zone. Germany conquered France by June 1940, forcing the French government to surrender. Britain, under Prime Minister Winston Churchill, stood alone against German aggression. The Battle of Britain (July-October 1940) was a major aerial conflict in which the Royal Air Force defeated the German Luftwaffe, preventing a German invasion. In 1941, Germany attacked the Soviet Union in Operation Barbarossa, opening up a massive new front. The United States, initially neutral, entered the war on December 7, 1941, following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor.

## The Pacific Theater

Japan sought to expand its empire across East Asia and the Pacific. The attack on Pearl Harbor killed over 1,100 American service members and destroyed numerous ships and aircraft. This attack brought the United States into World War II. Major battles in the Pacific included Midway, Guadalcanal, Iwo Jima, and Okinawa. Island-hopping was the strategy the United States used to move closer to Japan while bypassing heavily defended islands.

## The Holocaust

The Holocaust was the systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews by the Nazi regime. Additionally, millions of others, including Roma, disabled people, political prisoners, and Soviet prisoners of war, were also killed in concentration and extermination camps. The Holocaust represents one of history's greatest atrocities and is a stark reminder of the dangers of hatred and authoritarianism.

## Major Turning Points

Several events turned the tide of the war. The Battle of Stalingrad (1942-1943) was a brutal Soviet victory that halted German expansion in the East. The Battle of Midway (1942) was a turning point in the Pacific, as the U.S. Navy defeated the Japanese fleet. D-Day (June 6, 1944) was the Allied invasion of Normandy in France, marking the beginning of the liberation of Western Europe. The successful invasion established a foothold for the Allies in Nazi-occupied territory.

## The End of the War

Germany surrendered on May 7, 1945, following Hitler's suicide and the Soviet capture of Berlin. Japan initially refused to surrender despite the dire military situation. The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima on August 6, 1945, and on Nagasaki on August 9, 1945. Japan formally surrendered on August 15, 1945, ending World War II.

## Key Leaders

Major political and military leaders included Adolf Hitler (Nazi Germany), Benito Mussolini (Italy), Winston Churchill (Britain), Franklin D. Roosevelt (United States), Josef Stalin (Soviet Union), and Emperor Hirohito (Japan). Each leader's decisions shaped the course and outcome of the war.

## The War's Consequences

World War II reshaped the world. Europe lay in ruins and needed reconstruction through the Marshall Plan. The United Nations was founded in 1945 to promote international peace and security. The Cold War emerged as tensions between the Soviet Union and the Western powers grew. Germany and Japan were occupied and demilitarized. The war accelerated the process of decolonization in Asia and Africa as weakened European powers could no longer maintain their empires.
