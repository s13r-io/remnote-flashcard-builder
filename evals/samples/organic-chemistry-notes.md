# Organic Chemistry: Fundamentals

## What is Organic Chemistry?

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that studies the structure, properties, and reactions of carbon-containing compounds. Carbon is unique because it can form four covalent bonds and create long chains, branched structures, and rings. Organic compounds are found in all living organisms and include carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. Friedrich Wöhler is credited with founding organic chemistry after synthesizing urea from inorganic materials in 1828.

## Chemical Bonding in Organic Molecules

Carbon atoms form bonds through hybridization. In sp3 hybridization, carbon forms four single bonds arranged in a tetrahedral geometry with bond angles of approximately 109.5°. In sp2 hybridization, carbon forms a double bond and two single bonds in a trigonal planar geometry with bond angles of 120°. In sp hybridization, carbon forms a triple bond and one single bond in a linear geometry with bond angles of 180°. Sigma bonds (σ) result from head-on orbital overlap, while pi bonds (π) result from lateral orbital overlap.

## Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds composed only of carbon and hydrogen. Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with only single bonds, following the formula CnH2n+2. Alkenes contain at least one carbon-carbon double bond, following the formula CnH2n. Alkynes contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond, following the formula CnH2n-2. Aromatic hydrocarbons contain a benzene ring, a six-carbon ring with alternating double bonds and special stability due to electron delocalization.

## Functional Groups

Functional groups are specific groups of atoms that determine the chemical properties of an organic molecule. The hydroxyl group (-OH) defines alcohols. The carbonyl group (C=O) is found in aldehydes (terminal) and ketones (internal). The carboxyl group (-COOH) defines carboxylic acids. The amino group (-NH2) defines amines. The ester group (-COO-) is formed from the reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol. Each functional group gives the molecule distinct chemical reactivity.

## Isomerism

Isomers are molecules with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements. Structural isomers have different connectivity of atoms. Stereoisomers have the same connectivity but different spatial arrangements. Geometric isomers (cis-trans) occur around double bonds or ring structures: cis places groups on the same side, trans places them on opposite sides. Enantiomers are mirror-image stereoisomers that are non-superimposable, like left and right hands. A chiral center is a carbon atom bonded to four different groups.

## Organic Reactions

There are four main types of organic reactions. Addition reactions add atoms across a double or triple bond. Substitution reactions replace one atom or group with another. Elimination reactions remove atoms to form a double bond. Oxidation-reduction reactions involve the transfer of electrons. Markovnikov's rule states that in the addition of HX to an alkene, hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms already attached.

## Nomenclature

IUPAC nomenclature provides a systematic method for naming organic compounds. The parent chain is the longest continuous chain of carbon atoms. The suffix indicates the functional group: -ane for alkanes, -ene for alkenes, -yne for alkynes, -ol for alcohols, -al for aldehydes, -one for ketones, and -oic acid for carboxylic acids. Substituents are named with prefixes such as methyl (CH3-), ethyl (C2H5-), and numbered by their position on the parent chain.

## Polymers

Polymers are large molecules made of repeating smaller units called monomers. Addition polymers form when monomers with double bonds join together, such as polyethylene from ethylene. Condensation polymers form when monomers join by releasing a small molecule like water, such as nylon and polyester. Natural polymers include cellulose, starch, proteins, and DNA. Synthetic polymers include plastics, rubber, and fibers used in everyday products.
