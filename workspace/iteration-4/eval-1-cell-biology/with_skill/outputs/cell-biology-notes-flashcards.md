# Cell Biology: The Basics

## What is a Cell?

- **Cell** :: The basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms
- **All living organisms are composed of:** >> One or more cells
- **Prokaryotic cells** ;; Lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
- **Eukaryotic cells** ;; Contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
- **Which cell type lacks a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles?** >>A) Prokaryotic cells B) Eukaryotic cells C) Both D) Neither
- **Which cell type contains membrane-bound organelles?** >>A) Prokaryotic B) Eukaryotic C) Both D) Neither

## The Cell Membrane

- **Cell membrane** :: A thin, flexible barrier that surrounds the cell and separates its interior from the external environment; also called the plasma membrane
- **Plasma membrane** <> Cell membrane
- **Phospholipid bilayer** :: The primary structural component of the cell membrane
- **Hydrophobic tails in a phospholipid bilayer are oriented:** >> Inward (toward each other)
- **Hydrophilic heads in a phospholipid bilayer are oriented:** >> Outward (toward the external environment)
- **What controls what enters and exits the cell?** >> The cell membrane
- **Selectively permeable** ;; A property of the cell membrane that allows only certain molecules to pass through
- **The cell membrane is composed primarily of:**  >>>
Phospholipid bilayer
Embedded proteins

## Mitochondria

- **Mitochondria** :: Organelles responsible for producing ATP through cellular respiration; known as the powerhouse of the cell
- **ATP** << Adenosine triphosphate; energy molecule produced in mitochondria
- **Mitochondria have how many membranes?** >> Two: an outer membrane and an inner membrane
- **Cristae** ;; Folded structures of the inner mitochondrial membrane that increase surface area for chemical reactions
- **Cristae are located in the:** >> Inner membrane of mitochondria
- **Ancient origin of mitochondria suggested by:** >>> 
Own DNA
Own ribosomes
Similarity to bacteria

- **Which organelle produces ATP?** >>A) Mitochondria B) Ribosome C) Golgi apparatus D) Lysosome
- **The process by which mitochondria produce ATP is called:** >> Cellular respiration

## The Nucleus

- **Nucleus** :: The largest organelle in eukaryotic cells; contains the cell's genetic material
- **Genetic material contained in the nucleus is primarily:** >> DNA
- **Chromosomes** :: Structures formed by organized DNA within the nucleus
- **Nuclear envelope** :: A double membrane surrounding the nucleus with pores for molecule passage
- **Pores in the nuclear envelope:** >> Allow certain molecules to pass between nucleus and cytoplasm
- **DNA in the nucleus is {{transcribed}} into RNA**
- **RNA is {{translated}} into proteins in the cytoplasm**
- **What is the largest organelle in eukaryotic cells?** >>A) Ribosome B) Nucleus C) Mitochondria D) Golgi apparatus

## Ribosomes and Protein Synthesis

- **Ribosomes** :: Structures where proteins are synthesized by reading mRNA and translating genetic code into amino acid chains
- **mRNA stands for:** >> Messenger RNA
- **Function of ribosomes is to:** >> Read mRNA and translate the genetic code into chains of amino acids
- **Ribosomes can be found {{free-floating}} in the cytoplasm or {{attached}} to the endoplasmic reticulum**
- **Where ribosomes are attached to the endoplasmic reticulum, that region is called:** >> Rough endoplasmic reticulum
- **Protein synthesis occurs at:** >> Ribosomes

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum

- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** :: A network of membranous sacs and tubes involved in synthesis and transport
- **Rough endoplasmic reticulum** ;; Has ribosomes attached and is involved in protein synthesis
- **Smooth endoplasmic reticulum** ;; Lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification
- **Rough ER is involved in:** >> Protein synthesis
- **Smooth ER is involved in:** >>>
Lipid synthesis
Detoxification

- **Which type of endoplasmic reticulum has ribosomes attached?** >>A) Rough ER B) Smooth ER C) Both D) Neither
- **Which type of endoplasmic reticulum is involved in detoxification?** >>A) Rough ER B) Smooth ER C) Both D) Neither

## The Golgi Apparatus

- **Golgi apparatus** :: A membrane-bound organelle that modifies, packages, and ships proteins and lipids
- **Cisternae** ;; Stacked, flattened sacs that make up the Golgi apparatus
- **Primary function of the Golgi apparatus is to:** >>> 
Modify proteins and lipids
Package them
Ship them to their destination

- **Proteins arrive at the Golgi apparatus in:** >> Vesicles from the rough ER
- **After modification in the Golgi, proteins are:** >> Packaged into vesicles that bud off and travel to their final destination
- **The Golgi apparatus modifies and packages products from which organelle?** >>A) Mitochondria B) Nucleus C) Rough ER D) Lysosomes

## Lysosomes

- **Lysosomes** :: Small, round organelles filled with digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and cellular debris
- **Lysosomes are known as the:** >> "Garbage disposal" of the cell
- **Enzymes in lysosomes work best in {{acidic}} conditions**
- **What do lysosomal enzymes break down?** >>>
Proteins
Lipids
Carbohydrates
Nucleic acids

- **Which organelle acts as the garbage disposal of the cell?** >>A) Golgi apparatus B) Lysosome C) Mitochondria D) Ribosome
- **Lysosomal enzymes can break down:** >>A) Only proteins B) Only lipids C) Proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and nucleic acids D) Only carbohydrates

## The Cytoskeleton

- **Cytoskeleton** :: A network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape and enables movement
- **Functions of the cytoskeleton:** >>>
Support cell shape
Enable cell movement
Enable movement of organelles within cell

- **Microfilaments** ;; Made of actin protein; component of the cytoskeleton
- **Actin** <> Protein that makes up microfilaments
- **Intermediate filaments** ;; Made of various proteins; component of the cytoskeleton
- **Microtubules** ;; Made of tubulin protein; component of the cytoskeleton
- **Tubulin** <> Protein that makes up microtubules
- **The three main types of cytoskeletal filaments are:** >>>
Microfilaments
Intermediate filaments
Microtubules

- **Which protein makes up microfilaments?** >>A) Tubulin B) Actin C) Collagen D) Keratin
- **What gives the cell its shape?** >>A) Cell membrane B) Nucleus C) Cytoskeleton D) Ribosomes

## Cell Division

- **Cell division** :: The process by which a parent cell divides to produce two daughter cells
- **Two main types of cell division:** >>>
Mitosis
Meiosis

- **Mitosis** :: Cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells; used for growth and repair
- **Meiosis** :: Cell division that produces four genetically diverse daughter cells with half the chromosome number; used for sexual reproduction
- **Mitosis produces {{two}} genetically {{identical}} daughter cells**
- **Meiosis produces {{four}} genetically {{diverse}} daughter cells**
- **Which type of cell division is used for growth and repair?** >>A) Mitosis B) Meiosis C) Binary fission D) Budding
- **Which type of cell division is used for sexual reproduction?** >>A) Mitosis B) Meiosis C) Binary fission D) Budding
- **Daughter cells from meiosis have:**  >> Half the chromosome number of the parent cell
