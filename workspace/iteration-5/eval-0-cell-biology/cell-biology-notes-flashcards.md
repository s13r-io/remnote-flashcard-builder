# Cell Biology: The Basics

## What is a Cell?

- **Cell** :: The basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms
- All living things are composed of one or more {{cells}}.
- **What are the two main types of cells?** >> Prokaryotic cells and eukaryotic cells
- **Prokaryotic cells** ;; Lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
- **Eukaryotic cells** ;; Contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
- **Which type of cell lacks a nucleus?** >>A) Prokaryotic B) Eukaryotic C) Somatic D) Stem

## The Cell Membrane

- **Cell membrane** :: A thin, flexible barrier that surrounds the cell and separates its interior from the external environment
- **Cell membrane** <> Plasma membrane
- The cell membrane is composed primarily of a {{phospholipid bilayer}} with embedded proteins.
- **Phospholipid bilayer** :: Two layers of phospholipids with their hydrophobic tails facing inward and hydrophilic heads facing outward
- **In the phospholipid bilayer, which direction do the hydrophobic tails face?** >> Inward
- **In the phospholipid bilayer, which direction do the hydrophilic heads face?** >> Outward
- **What property of the cell membrane allows it to control what enters and exits the cell?** >> Selective permeability
- **Which part of the cell membrane faces inward: hydrophobic tails or hydrophilic heads?** >>A) Hydrophobic tails B) Hydrophilic heads C) Both face inward D) Neither faces inward

## Mitochondria

- **Mitochondria** :: Organelles responsible for producing ATP through the process of cellular respiration
- **What do mitochondria produce?** >> ATP through cellular respiration
- **How many membranes do mitochondria have?** >> Two: an outer membrane and an inner membrane
- **Cristae** :: Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane that increase the surface area for chemical reactions during cellular respiration
- **Why are the inner membranes of mitochondria folded into cristae?** >> To increase the surface area for chemical reactions during cellular respiration
- Each mitochondrion contains its own {{DNA}} and ribosomes.
- **What does the presence of their own DNA and ribosomes in mitochondria suggest about their origin?** >> They originated from ancient bacteria
- **Given: organelle with its own DNA, ribosomes, and double membrane resembling a bacterium** << Mitochondria

## The Nucleus

- **Nucleus** :: The largest organelle in eukaryotic cells, containing the cell's genetic material
- The cell's genetic material (DNA) is organized into structures called {{chromosomes}}.
- **Nuclear envelope** :: A double membrane surrounding the nucleus that has pores allowing certain molecules to pass through
- **What is the function of pores in the nuclear envelope?** >> Allowing certain molecules to pass through
- **What process converts DNA into RNA inside the nucleus?** >> Transcription
- **Where is RNA translated into proteins?** >> In the cytoplasm
- **Which organelle is the largest in eukaryotic cells?** >>A) Nucleus B) Mitochondria C) Golgi apparatus D) Endoplasmic reticulum

## Ribosomes and Protein Synthesis

- **Ribosomes** :: Structures where proteins are synthesized by reading mRNA and translating the genetic code into chains of amino acids
- **mRNA** <> Messenger RNA
- **What do ribosomes translate into chains of amino acids?** >> The genetic code carried by messenger RNA (mRNA)
- **Name the two locations where ribosomes can be found** >>>
Cytoplasm (free-floating)
Attached to the endoplasmic reticulum
- **Rough endoplasmic reticulum** ;; Named for the region of the endoplasmic reticulum where ribosomes are attached

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum

- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** :: A network of membranous sacs and tubes
- **ER** <> Endoplasmic reticulum
- **Rough endoplasmic reticulum** :: Has ribosomes attached and is involved in protein synthesis
- **Smooth endoplasmic reticulum** :: Lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification
- **Which type of ER is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification?** >>A) Smooth ER B) Rough ER C) Both equally D) Neither
- **What distinguishes rough ER from smooth ER structurally?** >> Rough ER has ribosomes attached to its surface; smooth ER lacks ribosomes

## The Golgi Apparatus

- **Golgi apparatus** :: A membrane-bound organelle consisting of stacked, flattened sacs called cisternae that modifies, packages, and ships proteins and lipids
- **Cisternae** ;; Stacked, flattened sacs that make up the Golgi apparatus
- **What are the three main functions of the Golgi apparatus?** >>>
Modify proteins and lipids
Package proteins and lipids
Ship proteins and lipids to their final destination
- Proteins arrive at the Golgi apparatus in {{vesicles}} from the rough ER.
- **Describe the pathway of a protein from the ER to its final destination** >> Proteins arrive in vesicles from rough ER, are modified in the Golgi, then packaged into vesicles that bud off and travel to their final destination
- **Given: organelle that modifies, packages, and ships proteins received from the ER** << Golgi apparatus

## Lysosomes

- **Lysosomes** :: Small, round organelles filled with digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and cellular debris
- **What conditions do lysosomal enzymes work best in?** >> Acidic conditions
- **What four types of macromolecules can lysosomal enzymes break down?** >>>
Proteins
Lipids
Carbohydrates
Nucleic acids
- **What is the function of lysosomes in the cell?** >> Breaking down waste materials and cellular debris using digestive enzymes

## The Cytoskeleton

- **Cytoskeleton** :: A network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape and enables movement
- **What are the three main types of cytoskeletal filaments?** >>>
Microfilaments (made of actin)
Intermediate filaments (made of various proteins)
Microtubules (made of tubulin)
- Microfilaments are made of the protein {{actin}}.
- Microtubules are made of the protein {{tubulin}}.
- **Intermediate filaments** ;; Made of various proteins, one of the three main cytoskeletal filament types
- **What three functions does the cytoskeleton perform?** >>>
Supports the cell
Allows the cell to move
Enables movement of organelles within the cell
- **Which cytoskeletal filament is made of actin?** >>A) Microfilaments B) Intermediate filaments C) Microtubules D) Myofilaments

## Cell Division

- **Cell division** :: The process by which a parent cell divides to produce two daughter cells
- **Mitosis** ;; Produces two genetically identical daughter cells; used for growth and repair
- **Meiosis** ;; Produces four genetically diverse daughter cells with half the chromosome number; used for sexual reproduction
- **How many daughter cells does mitosis produce?** >> Two
- **How many daughter cells does meiosis produce?** >> Four
- {{Meiosis}} is the type of cell division used for sexual reproduction.
- **Which type of cell division produces genetically identical daughter cells?** >>A) Mitosis B) Meiosis C) Binary fission D) Budding
- **Given: cell division producing four genetically diverse cells with half the chromosome number** << Meiosis
