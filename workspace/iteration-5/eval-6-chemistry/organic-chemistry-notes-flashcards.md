# Organic Chemistry: Fundamentals

## What is Organic Chemistry?

- **Organic chemistry** :: The branch of chemistry that studies the structure, properties, and reactions of carbon-containing compounds
- **How many covalent bonds can carbon form?** >> Four
- Carbon can create {{long chains}}, branched structures, and rings due to its bonding versatility.
- **Who is credited with founding organic chemistry?** >> Friedrich Wöhler
- Friedrich Wöhler synthesized {{urea}} from inorganic materials, founding organic chemistry.
- **In what year did Friedrich Wöhler synthesize urea from inorganic materials?** >> 1828
- **Four major classes of organic compounds in living organisms** >>>
Carbohydrates
Proteins
Lipids
Nucleic acids

## Chemical Bonding in Organic Molecules

- **Hybridization** :: The process by which carbon atoms form bonds by mixing atomic orbitals into new hybrid orbitals
- **sp3 hybridization** ;; Four single bonds, tetrahedral geometry, bond angles of approximately 109.5°
- **sp2 hybridization** ;; One double bond and two single bonds, trigonal planar geometry, bond angles of 120°
- **sp hybridization** ;; One triple bond and one single bond, linear geometry, bond angles of 180°
- **Which hybridization produces a tetrahedral geometry?** >>A) sp3 B) sp2 C) sp D) sp3d
- **Sigma bond (σ)** :: A bond resulting from head-on orbital overlap
- **Pi bond (π)** :: A bond resulting from lateral orbital overlap
- The bond angle in {{sp3}} hybridized carbon is approximately 109.5°.
- The bond angle in {{sp2}} hybridized carbon is approximately 120°.
- The bond angle in {{sp}} hybridized carbon is 180°.

## Hydrocarbons

- **Hydrocarbons** :: Organic compounds composed only of carbon and hydrogen
- **Alkanes** :: Saturated hydrocarbons with only single bonds, following the formula CnH2n+2
- **Alkenes** :: Hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond, following the formula CnH2n
- **Alkynes** :: Hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond, following the formula CnH2n-2
- **Which type of hydrocarbon is described as "saturated"?** >>A) Alkanes B) Alkenes C) Alkynes D) Aromatics
- **What is the general formula for alkanes?** >> CnH2n+2
- **What is the general formula for alkenes?** >> CnH2n
- **What is the general formula for alkynes?** >> CnH2n-2
- **Aromatic hydrocarbons** ;; Contain a benzene ring, a six-carbon ring with alternating double bonds and special stability due to electron delocalization
- **What gives aromatic hydrocarbons their special stability?** >> Electron delocalization in the benzene ring

## Functional Groups

- **Functional groups** :: Specific groups of atoms that determine the chemical properties of an organic molecule
- **-OH** <> Hydroxyl group
- **-NH2** <> Amino group
- **-COOH** <> Carboxyl group
- **-COO-** <> Ester group
- **C=O** <> Carbonyl group
- The hydroxyl group (-OH) defines {{alcohols}}.
- The carboxyl group (-COOH) defines {{carboxylic acids}}.
- The amino group (-NH2) defines {{amines}}.
- **Where is the carbonyl group (C=O) found in aldehydes vs. ketones?** >> Terminal position in aldehydes, internal position in ketones
- **How is an ester group formed?** >> From the reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol
- **Which functional group defines alcohols?** >>A) -OH B) -NH2 C) -COOH D) C=O

## Isomerism

- **Isomers** :: Molecules with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements
- **Structural isomers** :: Isomers with different connectivity of atoms
- **Stereoisomers** :: Isomers with the same connectivity but different spatial arrangements
- **Enantiomers** :: Mirror-image stereoisomers that are non-superimposable, like left and right hands
- **Chiral center** :: A carbon atom bonded to four different groups
- **Geometric isomers (cis-trans)** ;; Occur around double bonds or ring structures
- **cis isomer** ;; Groups placed on the same side of a double bond or ring
- **trans isomer** ;; Groups placed on opposite sides of a double bond or ring
- **Which type of isomer has the same connectivity but different spatial arrangement?** >>A) Stereoisomers B) Structural isomers C) Tautomers D) Constitutional isomers
- **Describe: non-superimposable mirror-image molecules** << Enantiomers

## Organic Reactions

- **Four main types of organic reactions** >>>
Addition reactions
Substitution reactions
Elimination reactions
Oxidation-reduction reactions
- **Addition reactions** :: Reactions that add atoms across a double or triple bond
- **Substitution reactions** :: Reactions that replace one atom or group with another
- **Elimination reactions** :: Reactions that remove atoms to form a double bond
- **Oxidation-reduction reactions** ;; Involve the transfer of electrons
- **Markovnikov's rule** :: In the addition of HX to an alkene, hydrogen adds to the carbon with more hydrogen atoms already attached
- **Which reaction type removes atoms to form a double bond?** >>A) Elimination B) Addition C) Substitution D) Redox
- **Describe: atoms are added across a double or triple bond** << Addition reaction

## Nomenclature

- **IUPAC nomenclature** :: A systematic method for naming organic compounds
- **Parent chain** ;; The longest continuous chain of carbon atoms in IUPAC nomenclature
- **IUPAC suffixes for common functional groups** >>>
-ane for alkanes
-ene for alkenes
-yne for alkynes
-ol for alcohols
-al for aldehydes
-one for ketones
-oic acid for carboxylic acids
- **-ane** <> Alkane suffix
- **-ene** <> Alkene suffix
- **-yne** <> Alkyne suffix
- **-ol** <> Alcohol suffix
- **-al** <> Aldehyde suffix
- **-one** <> Ketone suffix
- **-oic acid** <> Carboxylic acid suffix
- The prefix {{methyl}} refers to the substituent CH3-.
- The prefix {{ethyl}} refers to the substituent C2H5-.
- **How are substituents numbered in IUPAC nomenclature?** >> By their position on the parent chain

## Polymers

- **Polymers** :: Large molecules made of repeating smaller units called monomers
- **Monomers** :: The repeating smaller units that make up polymers
- **Addition polymers** ;; Form when monomers with double bonds join together (e.g., polyethylene from ethylene)
- **Condensation polymers** ;; Form when monomers join by releasing a small molecule like water (e.g., nylon and polyester)
- **Which polymer type releases a small molecule like water during formation?** >>A) Condensation polymers B) Addition polymers C) Copolymers D) Cross-linked polymers
- **Examples of natural polymers** >>>
Cellulose
Starch
Proteins
DNA
- **Examples of synthetic polymers** >>>
Plastics
Rubber
Fibers
- {{Polyethylene}} is an addition polymer made from ethylene monomers.
- **Describe: polymers formed by releasing a small molecule such as water** << Condensation polymers
