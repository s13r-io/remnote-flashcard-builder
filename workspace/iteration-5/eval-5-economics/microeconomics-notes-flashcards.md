# Microeconomics: Core Principles

## What is Microeconomics?

- **Microeconomics** :: The branch of economics that studies the behavior of individuals, households, and firms in making decisions regarding the allocation of scarce resources
- **Which branch of economics examines the economy as a whole rather than specific markets?** >> Macroeconomics
- **What does microeconomics focus on, unlike macroeconomics?** >> Specific markets and individual agents
- **Scarcity** :: The fundamental problem in economics: unlimited wants but limited resources
- **What distinguishes microeconomics from macroeconomics?** >>A) Microeconomics studies individual markets; macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole B) Microeconomics studies national output; macroeconomics studies firms C) Microeconomics studies inflation; macroeconomics studies individual behavior D) Microeconomics studies trade policy; macroeconomics studies consumer choice

## Supply and Demand

- **Law of demand** :: All else being equal, as the price of a good increases, the quantity demanded decreases
- **Law of supply** :: All else being equal, as the price increases, the quantity supplied increases
- **In which direction does the demand curve slope?** >> Downward from left to right
- **In which direction does the supply curve slope?** >> Upward from left to right
- **Market equilibrium** :: The point where the supply and demand curves intersect, determining the equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity
- The {{law of demand}} states that as price increases, quantity demanded decreases, all else being equal.
- **Upward-sloping curve on a price-quantity graph** << Supply curve
- **What two values are determined at market equilibrium?** >> Equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity
- **Which curve slopes downward: supply or demand?** >>A) Demand B) Supply C) Both slope downward D) Neither slopes downward

## Elasticity

- **Price elasticity of demand** :: A measure of how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in price
- **How is price elasticity of demand calculated?** >> Percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price
- **Elastic demand** ;; Elasticity greater than 1; consumers are highly responsive to price changes
- **Inelastic demand** ;; Elasticity less than 1; consumers are relatively unresponsive to price changes
- **Unit elastic demand** ;; Elasticity of exactly 1
- **What type of goods tend to have inelastic demand?** >> Necessities
- **What type of goods tend to have elastic demand?** >> Luxury goods
- **Demand with elasticity greater than 1** << Elastic demand
- **Which type of demand describes consumers who are relatively unresponsive to price changes?** >>A) Inelastic demand B) Elastic demand C) Unit elastic demand D) Perfectly elastic demand
- The price elasticity threshold separating elastic from inelastic demand is {{1}}.

## Consumer Surplus and Producer Surplus

- **Consumer surplus** :: The difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay
- **Producer surplus** :: The difference between the market price and the minimum price producers are willing to accept
- **How is consumer surplus represented graphically?** >> The area between the demand curve and the market price
- **How is producer surplus represented graphically?** >> The area between the supply curve and the market price
- **Total surplus** ;; The sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus; also called economic welfare
- **Economic welfare** <> Total surplus
- **Area between the supply curve and the market price on a graph** << Producer surplus

## Market Structures

- **How many main market structures are there?** >> Four
- **Name the four main market structures** >>>
Perfect competition
Monopoly
Monopolistic competition
Oligopoly
- **Perfect competition** :: A market structure with many buyers and sellers, identical products, and no barriers to entry
- **Monopoly** :: A market structure with a single seller, no close substitutes, and high barriers to entry
- **Monopolistic competition** :: A market structure with many sellers, differentiated products, and low barriers to entry
- **Oligopoly** :: A market structure with a few large sellers that dominate the market and high barriers to entry
- **Price takers** ;; Firms in perfect competition that must accept the market price
- **Price makers** ;; Monopolists that can set their own price
- **Which market structure features a single seller with no close substitutes?** >>A) Monopoly B) Oligopoly C) Perfect competition D) Monopolistic competition
- **Which market structure has many sellers but differentiated products?** >>A) Monopolistic competition B) Perfect competition C) Oligopoly D) Monopoly
- **Firms that must accept the market price because they have no market power** << Price takers (firms in perfect competition)
- **Many sellers, differentiated products, low barriers to entry** << Monopolistic competition

## Costs of Production

- **Fixed costs** :: Costs that do not change with the level of output (e.g., rent and insurance)
- **Variable costs** :: Costs that change as output changes (e.g., raw materials and labor)
- **Total cost formula** ;; TC = FC + VC (fixed costs plus variable costs)
- **Marginal cost** :: The additional cost of producing one more unit of output
- **Average total cost** ;; Total cost divided by the quantity of output (ATC = TC/Q)
- **Economies of scale** :: When average total cost decreases as output increases
- The formula for average total cost is ATC = {{TC/Q}}.
- The total cost formula is TC = {{FC + VC}}.
- **Which cost type includes rent and insurance?** >>A) Fixed costs B) Variable costs C) Marginal costs D) Opportunity costs
- **Cost that changes as output changes, such as raw materials** << Variable costs

## Profit Maximization

- **At what point do firms maximize profit?** >> Where marginal revenue equals marginal cost (MR = MC)
- **In perfect competition, what does marginal revenue equal?** >> The market price
- **Economic profit** ;; Earned when total revenue exceeds total cost, including opportunity costs
- **Normal profit** ;; Occurs when total revenue equals total cost
- **When should a firm shut down in the short run?** >> When the price falls below average variable cost
- Firms maximize profit by producing where {{marginal revenue}} equals marginal cost.
- **A firm earning zero economic profit, where total revenue equals total cost** << Normal profit
- **What is the shutdown condition for a firm in the short run?** >>A) Price falls below average variable cost B) Price falls below average total cost C) Price falls below marginal cost D) Price equals marginal revenue

## Market Failures and Externalities

- **Market failure** :: When the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently
- **Externalities** :: Costs or benefits that affect third parties not involved in a transaction
- **Negative externality** ;; Results in overproduction because social costs exceed private costs (e.g., pollution)
- **Positive externality** ;; Results in underproduction because social benefits exceed private benefits (e.g., education)
- **What is an example of a negative externality?** >> Pollution
- **What is an example of a positive externality?** >> Education
- **Name three government interventions to correct externalities** >>>
Taxes
Subsidies
Regulations
- **Overproduction due to social costs exceeding private costs** << Negative externality
- **Which type of externality leads to underproduction?** >>A) Positive externality B) Negative externality C) Both types equally D) Neither type
- {{Negative}} externalities such as pollution result in overproduction, while {{positive}} externalities such as education result in underproduction.
