# Physics: Classical Mechanics

## What is Mechanics?

- **Mechanics** :: The branch of physics that deals with the motion of objects and the forces that cause or change that motion
- **Kinematics** :: The area of mechanics that describes motion without considering its causes
- **Dynamics** :: The area of mechanics that studies the forces that produce motion
- **What are the two main areas of mechanics?** >> Kinematics and dynamics
- Classical mechanics applies to objects moving at speeds much slower than the speed of {{light}}.
- **Which area of mechanics considers the causes of motion: kinematics or dynamics?** >>A) Dynamics B) Kinematics C) Thermodynamics D) Optics

## Newton's Three Laws of Motion

- **Who formulated the three laws of motion?** >> Sir Isaac Newton
- Newton published his three laws of motion in {{1687}}.
- **Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia)** :: An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted upon by a net external force
- **Newton's Second Law** :: The net force acting on an object equals its mass times its acceleration (F = ma)
- **Newton's Third Law** :: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction
- **How many fundamental laws of motion did Newton formulate?** >> Three
- **Law of Inertia** <> Newton's First Law
- **Which law states that F = ma?** >>A) Newton's Second Law B) Newton's First Law C) Newton's Third Law D) Law of Conservation of Energy

## Force and Weight

- **Force** :: A push or pull that can change an object's motion
- Force is measured in units called {{newtons}} (N).
- One newton equals one kilogram times one meter per second squared ({{1 N = 1 kg·m/s²}}).
- **Weight** :: The force of gravity acting on an object's mass
- **Formula for weight** ;; W = mg, where g is the acceleration due to gravity
- The acceleration due to gravity on Earth is approximately {{9.8 m/s²}}.
- **What is the difference between mass and weight?** >> Mass is a measure of the amount of matter in an object (in kilograms), while weight depends on the gravitational field
- **measured in kilograms and does not change with location** << Mass
- **Which quantity depends on the gravitational field: mass or weight?** >>A) Weight B) Mass C) Both equally D) Neither

## Friction

- **Friction** :: A force that opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact
- **Two main types of friction** >>>
Static friction
Kinetic friction
- **Static friction** ;; Prevents an object from starting to move
- **Kinetic friction** ;; Acts on an object already in motion
- **Which type of friction is generally greater: static or kinetic?** >> Static friction is generally greater than kinetic friction
- **Coefficient of friction (μ)** :: A dimensionless number that represents how much friction exists between two surfaces
- **opposes motion and acts on a moving object** << Kinetic friction

## Work and Energy

- **Work (in physics)** :: Done when a force causes an object to move in the direction of the force
- **Formula for work** ;; W = Fd cos θ, where F is force, d is displacement, and θ is the angle between them
- **Energy** :: The capacity to do work
- **Kinetic energy** :: The energy of motion
- **Formula for kinetic energy** ;; KE = ½mv²
- **Potential energy** :: Stored energy due to position
- **Gravitational potential energy formula** ;; PE = mgh
- **Work-energy theorem** :: The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy
- **What does θ represent in the work formula W = Fd cos θ?** >> The angle between the force and the displacement
- **What are the three variables in the work formula?** >>>
Force (F)
Displacement (d)
Angle between them (θ)

## Conservation of Energy

- **Law of conservation of energy** :: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another
- In a closed system, the total energy remains {{constant}}.
- **What happens to a falling object's energy?** >> Potential energy converts into kinetic energy
- **Mechanical energy** :: The sum of kinetic and potential energy in a system
- **What two forms of energy make up mechanical energy?** >>A) Kinetic and potential energy B) Thermal and chemical energy C) Nuclear and electrical energy D) Sound and light energy

## Momentum

- **Momentum** :: The product of an object's mass and velocity
- **Formula for momentum** ;; p = mv
- **Is momentum a scalar or vector quantity?** >> Vector quantity — it has both magnitude and direction
- **Law of conservation of momentum** :: In a closed system with no external forces, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after
- **Two types of collisions** >>>
Elastic collisions (kinetic energy is conserved)
Inelastic collisions (kinetic energy is not conserved)
- **Elastic collision** ;; A collision in which kinetic energy is conserved
- **Inelastic collision** ;; A collision in which kinetic energy is not conserved
- **In which type of collision is kinetic energy conserved?** >>A) Elastic B) Inelastic C) Both D) Neither

## Impulse

- **Impulse** :: The change in momentum of an object
- **Formula for impulse** ;; J = FΔt (force multiplied by the time interval)
- **Impulse-momentum theorem** :: Impulse equals the change in momentum (J = Δp)
- **Why do airbags reduce injury in car crashes?** >> They increase the time of impact, thereby reducing the force experienced by a passenger
- **J** <> Impulse
- The impulse-momentum theorem states that {{J = Δp}}.
