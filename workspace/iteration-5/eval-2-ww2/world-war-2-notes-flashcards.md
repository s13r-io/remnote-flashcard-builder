# World War II: Key Events and Facts

## What Started World War II?

- World War II began on {{September 1, 1939}}, when Nazi Germany invaded Poland.
- **Who led Nazi Germany when it invaded Poland?** >> Adolf Hitler
- **What event started World War II?** >> Germany's invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939
- **When did Britain and France declare war on Germany?** >> September 3, 1939, two days after Germany invaded Poland
- The deadliest conflict in human history resulted in approximately {{70-85 million}} deaths.
- **Which two countries declared war on Germany after the invasion of Poland?** >>A) Britain and France B) United States and Soviet Union C) Italy and Japan D) Spain and Portugal

## Key Causes of World War II

- **Treaty of Versailles** :: Post-World War I treaty signed in 1919 that imposed harsh penalties on Germany, creating resentment and economic hardship
- Economic depression in the {{1930s}} led to unemployment and social unrest across Europe.
- **Appeasement** :: Policy by Britain and France that allowed aggressive nations to expand unchecked before World War II
- **Which three authoritarian leaders rose to power and created militaristic regimes bent on expansion?** >>>
Adolf Hitler (Germany)
Benito Mussolini (Italy)
Hideki Tojo (Japan)
- **Economic depression in the 1930s led to which two social problems across Europe?** >> Unemployment and social unrest
- The {{Treaty of Versailles}} was signed in 1919 after World War I and imposed harsh penalties on Germany.
- **Which countries practiced appeasement before WWII?** >>A) Britain and France B) United States and Soviet Union C) Italy and Japan D) Poland and Czechoslovakia

## The European Theater

- **European Theater** :: The primary conflict zone of World War II
- Germany conquered {{France}} by June 1940, forcing its government to surrender.
- **Who was the British Prime Minister who led Britain against German aggression?** >> Winston Churchill
- **Battle of Britain** :: Major aerial conflict (July–October 1940) in which the Royal Air Force defeated the German Luftwaffe, preventing a German invasion of Britain
- **Operation Barbarossa** :: Germany's 1941 attack on the Soviet Union, opening a massive new Eastern Front
- **What was the U.S. stance before entering WWII?** >>A) Initially neutral B) Allied with Britain from the start C) Supporting Germany D) Engaged in a separate war with Japan
- **When did the United States enter World War II?** >> December 7, 1941, following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor
- **Which air force defeated the German Luftwaffe in the Battle of Britain?** << Royal Air Force
- **What did Britain's victory in the Battle of Britain prevent?** >> A German invasion of Britain
- **aerial conflict, July–October 1940, RAF vs. Luftwaffe** << Battle of Britain

## The Pacific Theater

- **Why did Japan seek expansion across East Asia and the Pacific?** >> To expand its empire
- The attack on {{Pearl Harbor}} killed over 1,100 American service members and destroyed numerous ships and aircraft.
- **How many American service members were killed in the attack on Pearl Harbor?** >> Over 1,100
- **What were the major battles in the Pacific Theater?** >>>
Midway
Guadalcanal
Iwo Jima
Okinawa
- **Island-hopping** :: U.S. military strategy of moving closer to Japan while bypassing heavily defended islands
- **What event brought the United States into World War II?** >> The Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor

## The Holocaust

- **Holocaust** :: The systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews by the Nazi regime
- The Holocaust resulted in the murder of {{six million}} Jews by the Nazi regime.
- **Besides Jews, which groups were also killed in concentration and extermination camps?** >>>
Roma
Disabled people
Political prisoners
Soviet prisoners of war
- **What types of camps were used to carry out the Holocaust?** >> Concentration and extermination camps
- **systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews** << Holocaust

## Major Turning Points

- **Battle of Stalingrad** :: Brutal Soviet victory (1942–1943) that halted German expansion in the East
- **Battle of Midway** :: 1942 turning point in the Pacific where the U.S. Navy defeated the Japanese fleet
- **D-Day** :: Allied invasion of Normandy, France on June 6, 1944, marking the beginning of the liberation of Western Europe
- **Which battle was the turning point that halted German expansion in the East?** << Battle of Stalingrad
- The Allied invasion of Normandy took place on {{June 6, 1944}}.
- **What did D-Day establish for the Allies?** >> A foothold in Nazi-occupied territory, beginning the liberation of Western Europe
- **In what year was the Battle of Midway?** >> 1942

## The End of the War

- Germany surrendered on {{May 7, 1945}}, following Hitler's suicide and the Soviet capture of Berlin.
- **What two events preceded Germany's surrender?** >> Hitler's suicide and the Soviet capture of Berlin
- The United States dropped an atomic bomb on {{Hiroshima}} on August 6, 1945.
- The United States dropped an atomic bomb on {{Nagasaki}} on August 9, 1945.
- **When did Japan formally surrender, ending World War II?** >> August 15, 1945
- **What finally compelled Japan to surrender?** >>A) The atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki B) A Soviet invasion of Japan C) A naval blockade D) An Allied invasion of the Japanese mainland

## The War's Consequences

- **Marshall Plan** :: Post-war reconstruction program for Europe, which lay in ruins after WWII
- **United Nations** :: International organization founded in 1945 to promote international peace and security
- **Cold War** :: Post-WWII tensions that emerged between the Soviet Union and the Western powers
- **What happened to Germany and Japan after the war?** >> They were occupied and demilitarized
- **Decolonization** ;; Accelerated after WWII as weakened European powers could no longer maintain their empires in Asia and Africa
- The {{United Nations}} was founded in 1945 to promote international peace and security.
