# Cell Biology Flashcards

## Foundational Concepts

### Card 1: Definition of a Cell
**Front:** What is a cell?
**Back:** A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms. All living things are composed of one or more cells, and cells come in two main types: prokaryotic cells (lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles) and eukaryotic cells (containing a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles).

### Card 2: Types of Cells
**Front:** What is the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?
**Back:** Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

---

## Cell Membrane

### Card 3: Cell Membrane Definition
**Front:** What is the cell membrane and what is its primary function?
**Back:** The cell membrane (also called plasma membrane) is a thin, flexible barrier that surrounds the cell and separates its interior from the external environment. Its primary function is to control what enters and exits the cell, making it selectively permeable.

### Card 4: Cell Membrane Composition
**Front:** What is the cell membrane primarily composed of?
**Back:** The cell membrane is composed primarily of a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins.

### Card 5: Phospholipid Bilayer Structure
**Front:** Describe the structure of a phospholipid bilayer.
**Back:** The phospholipid bilayer consists of two layers of phospholipids with their hydrophobic (water-repelling) tails facing inward and hydrophilic (water-attracting) heads facing outward.

---

## Mitochondria

### Card 6: Mitochondria Function
**Front:** What is the primary function of mitochondria?
**Back:** Mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell. They are responsible for producing ATP through the process of cellular respiration.

### Card 7: Mitochondrial Structure
**Front:** Describe the structure of mitochondria.
**Back:** Mitochondria have two membranes: an outer membrane and an inner membrane. The inner membrane is folded into structures called cristae, which increase the surface area for chemical reactions during cellular respiration.

### Card 8: Cristae Function
**Front:** What are cristae and why are they important?
**Back:** Cristae are folds in the inner mitochondrial membrane that increase the surface area available for the chemical reactions that occur during cellular respiration.

### Card 9: Mitochondrial DNA and Origin
**Front:** What does the presence of DNA and ribosomes in mitochondria suggest about their origin?
**Back:** Each mitochondrion contains its own DNA and ribosomes, which suggests they originated from ancient bacteria. This supports the endosymbiotic theory of cellular evolution.

---

## The Nucleus

### Card 10: Nucleus Definition
**Front:** What is the nucleus and what does it contain?
**Back:** The nucleus is the largest organelle in eukaryotic cells. It contains the cell's genetic material, primarily DNA, organized into structures called chromosomes.

### Card 11: Nuclear Envelope
**Front:** Describe the nuclear envelope and its function.
**Back:** The nucleus is surrounded by a nuclear envelope, which is a double membrane that has pores allowing certain molecules to pass through, enabling communication between the nucleus and cytoplasm.

### Card 12: Genetic Information Flow in the Nucleus
**Front:** What process occurs inside the nucleus involving DNA and RNA?
**Back:** Inside the nucleus, DNA is transcribed into RNA, which is then translated into proteins in the cytoplasm.

---

## Ribosomes and Protein Synthesis

### Card 13: Ribosome Function
**Front:** What is the primary function of ribosomes?
**Back:** Ribosomes are structures where proteins are synthesized. They read messenger RNA (mRNA) and translate the genetic code into chains of amino acids.

### Card 14: Ribosome Locations
**Front:** Where can ribosomes be found in the cell?
**Back:** Ribosomes can be found free-floating in the cytoplasm or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum.

### Card 15: Free vs. Bound Ribosomes
**Front:** What is the difference between free-floating ribosomes and bound ribosomes?
**Back:** Free-floating ribosomes are located in the cytoplasm and synthesize proteins for cytoplasmic use. Bound ribosomes are attached to the endoplasmic reticulum, forming rough endoplasmic reticulum, and synthesize proteins for secretion or membrane insertion.

---

## The Endoplasmic Reticulum

### Card 16: Endoplasmic Reticulum Definition
**Front:** What is the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)?
**Back:** The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranous sacs and tubes involved in various cellular functions.

### Card 17: Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum
**Front:** What is rough endoplasmic reticulum and what is its function?
**Back:** Rough endoplasmic reticulum has ribosomes attached to its surface and is involved in protein synthesis.

### Card 18: Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum
**Front:** What is smooth endoplasmic reticulum and what is its function?
**Back:** Smooth endoplasmic reticulum lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification.

### Card 19: Types of ER
**Front:** What are the two types of endoplasmic reticulum and what are their key differences?
**Back:** Rough ER has ribosomes attached and synthesizes proteins; Smooth ER lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and detoxification.

---

## The Golgi Apparatus

### Card 20: Golgi Apparatus Structure
**Front:** What is the structure of the Golgi apparatus?
**Back:** The Golgi apparatus is a membrane-bound organelle that consists of stacked, flattened sacs called cisternae.

### Card 21: Golgi Apparatus Function
**Front:** What is the primary function of the Golgi apparatus?
**Back:** The primary function of the Golgi apparatus is to modify, package, and ship proteins and lipids received from the ER.

### Card 22: Protein Processing in the Golgi
**Front:** Describe the process of protein modification and packaging in the Golgi apparatus.
**Back:** Proteins arrive in vesicles from the rough ER, are modified in the Golgi apparatus, and then are packaged into vesicles that bud off and travel to their final destination.

---

## Lysosomes

### Card 23: Lysosome Definition
**Front:** What are lysosomes?
**Back:** Lysosomes are small, round organelles filled with digestive enzymes.

### Card 24: Lysosome Function
**Front:** What is the function of lysosomes?
**Back:** Lysosomes function as the "garbage disposal" of the cell. Their digestive enzymes break down waste materials and cellular debris.

### Card 25: Lysosomal Enzymes
**Front:** What substances can the enzymes in lysosomes break down?
**Back:** The enzymes in lysosomes can break down proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and nucleic acids.

### Card 26: Lysosomal pH Preference
**Front:** What pH conditions do lysosomal enzymes work best in?
**Back:** The enzymes in lysosomes work best in acidic conditions, making lysosomes optimal garbage disposals in the cellular environment.

---

## The Cytoskeleton

### Card 27: Cytoskeleton Definition
**Front:** What is the cytoskeleton?
**Back:** The cytoskeleton is a network of protein filaments that gives the cell its shape and enables movement.

### Card 28: Functions of the Cytoskeleton
**Front:** What are the main functions of the cytoskeleton?
**Back:** The cytoskeleton supports the cell, allows it to move, and enables the movement of organelles within the cell.

### Card 29: Types of Cytoskeletal Filaments
**Front:** What are the three main types of filaments that make up the cytoskeleton?
**Back:** The three main types are: microfilaments (made of actin), intermediate filaments (made of various proteins), and microtubules (made of tubulin).

### Card 30: Microfilaments
**Front:** What are microfilaments made of and what do they do?
**Back:** Microfilaments are made of actin and are part of the cytoskeleton that provides structure and enables cell movement.

### Card 31: Intermediate Filaments
**Front:** What are intermediate filaments made of?
**Back:** Intermediate filaments are made of various proteins and provide structural support to the cell.

### Card 32: Microtubules
**Front:** What are microtubules made of and what is their role?
**Back:** Microtubules are made of tubulin and are part of the cytoskeleton that provides structure and enables the movement of organelles within the cell.

---

## Cell Division

### Card 33: Cell Division Definition
**Front:** What is cell division?
**Back:** Cell division is the process by which a parent cell divides to produce two daughter cells.

### Card 34: Types of Cell Division
**Front:** What are the two main types of cell division?
**Back:** The two main types of cell division are mitosis and meiosis.

### Card 35: Mitosis Definition and Function
**Front:** What is mitosis and what is its function?
**Back:** Mitosis is a type of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells and is used for growth and repair.

### Card 36: Meiosis Definition and Function
**Front:** What is meiosis and what is its function?
**Back:** Meiosis is a type of cell division that produces four genetically diverse daughter cells with half the chromosome number, and is used for sexual reproduction.

### Card 37: Mitosis vs. Meiosis - Products
**Front:** What is the key difference between the products of mitosis and meiosis?
**Back:** Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells, while meiosis produces four genetically diverse daughter cells with half the chromosome number.

### Card 38: Mitosis vs. Meiosis - Purpose
**Front:** What is the difference in purpose between mitosis and meiosis?
**Back:** Mitosis is used for growth and repair of the organism, while meiosis is used for sexual reproduction.

## Summary Review Cards

### Card 39: Major Organelles and Their Functions
**Front:** List the major organelles of eukaryotic cells and their primary functions.
**Back:**
- Nucleus: Contains genetic material (DNA)
- Mitochondria: Produces ATP through cellular respiration
- Rough ER: Protein synthesis
- Smooth ER: Lipid synthesis and detoxification
- Golgi Apparatus: Modifies and packages proteins and lipids
- Lysosomes: Breaks down waste and cellular debris
- Ribosomes: Translates mRNA into proteins

### Card 40: The Path of Protein Synthesis and Secretion
**Front:** Describe the pathway of protein synthesis and secretion from the rough ER to final destination.
**Back:** Proteins are synthesized by ribosomes on the rough ER, transported in vesicles to the Golgi apparatus, modified and packaged in the Golgi, then transported in vesicles to their final destination (cell membrane, lysosomes, etc.).
